Research Project

Spatial Reasoning with Graph Neural Networks

This project studies how graph-based models preserve global spatial structure from paired egocentric images, visible point-cloud subgraphs, and coarsened scene geometry.

Overview

The central question is whether visibility-aware geometric supervision can help learned representations capture global spatial structure. The repository currently contains a two-stage pipeline: first, cross-modal latent alignment between images and visible point-cloud graphs; second, localization of latent observations against a larger scene graph.

Visibility metrics are useful because they depend on long-range spatial relationships. This makes them a natural way to evaluate locality bias and global reasoning in graph neural networks.

Method

  1. Cross-modal autoencoding. Image and point-cloud branches encode paired observations into matching latent vectors, with reconstruction heads crossing between image and geometry.
  2. Random-walk observations. Synthetic trajectories generate RGB views, visible point-cloud slices, and camera-relative metadata for sequential latent supervision.
  3. Scene-graph localization. Latent observations are matched against cluster embeddings from a coarsened global point-cloud graph to predict translation and relative transforms.
